5 Free Mini Games You Can Play Right in Your Browser

Sometimes you don't want to download anything. You've got sixty seconds between meetings, or you're waiting for a page to load, or the wheel just hasn't given you a good enough excuse to keep procrastinating — you just want something quick, free, and immediately playable. No app store, no account, no install. Here's what's waiting in the Games tab.

Why a browser game beats an app-store game for this

App-store games want an install, permissions, and usually a login before you've even seen the gameplay. A browser game either works the moment the page loads or it doesn't belong on the internet. Every game here runs instantly, works the same on a phone or a laptop, saves your high score locally on your own device, and asks for nothing in return.

The five games

🎟️ Ticket Grab

The classic — a snake-style grid game where you steer with arrow keys, WASD, swipes, or an on-screen D-pad, growing longer with every ticket you grab. Levels ramp up gradually, so it starts easy and gets genuinely tense the longer you survive.

🎡 Wedge Dodge

A reflex test: colorful wedges fall from the top of the screen and you steer a cart left and right to avoid them. Drag anywhere on the board, or use the on-screen buttons — it speeds up the longer you last, so the real question is how long "long" turns out to be.

🔪 Ticket Slice

Swipe-to-slice tickets as they arc through the air, chain multiple slices together in one swipe for a combo bonus, and whatever you do — don't slice a bomb. One bad swipe ends the run instantly, which is exactly what makes the good runs feel so good.

👻 Ticket Muncher

A maze-chase game in the classic mold: clear every ticket in the maze while two inspectors hunt you down, grab a golden ticket to briefly turn the tables and eat them instead, and clear the whole board to advance to the next level — the maze refills and the inspectors get a little sharper each time.

🎈 Balloon Rise

Tap, click, or hit spacebar to keep a balloon aloft while gravity does its best to bring it down, threading gaps between scrolling poles that get tighter and faster the longer you keep it up.

They all work on mobile, properly

Every game here was built touch-first, not just touch-compatible — on a phone, starting a game automatically expands it to fill the screen, controls scale up, and nothing requires precision you can't manage with a thumb. Whichever one you open, it should feel like a mobile game, not a desktop game someone shrank down.

Pick one and see how long you actually last — high scores are saved right in your browser, so you've got something to beat next time.
